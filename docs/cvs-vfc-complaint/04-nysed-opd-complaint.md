## Statement of Complaint

**Licensee complained of:** Pharmacist on duty, CVS Pharmacy, 2833 Broadway, New York, NY 10025, on July 30, 2026, at approximately [time] ([name if known / physical description]). The pharmacy establishment itself is also identified for any action the State Board of Pharmacy deems appropriate.

**Complainant:** [Name], [address], [phone], zhang2qaz@gmail.com — parent of the minor patient.

**Facts.** On [date], staff at this pharmacy confirmed to me in person that the store participates in the federal Vaccines for Children (VFC) program. I then booked a vaccination appointment for my child ([age]) through CVS's online system for July 30, 2026 (confirmation available). At the appointment, the pharmacist/staff member on duty:

1. denied ever having heard of the VFC program and insisted I provide insurance or pay out of pocket for a childhood vaccination;
2. rudely cut off my attempt to explain the program, stated "there is no VFC here," and directed me to "contact VFC yourself and figure it out" before returning;
3. refused to vaccinate my child, performing no VFC eligibility screening of any kind; and
4. after I stepped away, loudly ridiculed my family to a coworker — audible in the public area of the store — as trying to get free vaccines.

**Grounds.** I believe this conduct constitutes professional misconduct under Education Law § 6509 and the Rules of the Board of Regents, including:

- **8 NYCRR 29.2(a)(2)** (applicable to pharmacy via 29.7): willfully harassing, abusing or intimidating a patient verbally — the contemptuous dismissal of a parent seeking a federally funded childhood immunization, and the loud public ridicule of the family, directed at and concerning a patient interaction;
- **8 NYCRR 29.1(b)**: conduct in the practice of the profession evidencing moral unfitness, and making representations tending to deceive — a licensed professional either (a) refused to honor VFC obligations applicable to an enrolled provider (no eligibility screening; demand for payment; refusal to vaccinate over payment), or (b) allowed a patient to rely on the store's affirmative confirmation of VFC participation and then denied the program's existence rather than making any effort to verify or to direct the family appropriately;
- To the extent the store is a VFC-enrolled provider, the refusal to vaccinate an eligible child over payment violates the CDC VFC provider agreement (administration fee must be waived on inability to pay; vaccination may not be refused for inability to pay), which I submit is relevant to the standard of professional conduct.

**Supporting materials available:** online appointment confirmation; contemporaneous written timeline; [witness statement]; correspondence with CVS corporate, the NYC DOHMH Bureau of Immunization (NYC VFC program authority), and the NY Attorney General Health Care Bureau, with whom parallel complaints have been filed.

**Relief sought.** Investigation of the licensee's conduct and whatever disciplinary action the Board of Regents deems warranted; I am available to provide testimony.
