I am writing to file a formal complaint regarding the conduct of pharmacy staff at the above store, and to request a written response with a case number.

**What happened.** On [date], I visited this store in person and asked whether it participates in the federal Vaccines for Children (VFC) program. A staff member confirmed that it does. Relying on that confirmation, I booked a vaccination appointment for my child through CVS's online scheduling system for July 30, 2026 (confirmation: [number/screenshot attached]). When we arrived for the appointment, the pharmacy staff member on duty ([name/description]):

1. stated she had never heard of VFC and insisted that I must provide insurance or pay out of pocket;
2. rudely cut me off when I attempted to explain what the VFC program is, and told me that "there is no VFC here" and that I should "contact VFC myself and figure it out" before coming back;
3. refused to vaccinate my child; and
4. after I stepped away, loudly mocked us to a coworker — within earshot of the public area — as trying to get free vaccines.

**Why this is a serious problem for CVS, not a routine service complaint.**

- VFC is a federal entitlement program under Section 1928 of the Social Security Act (42 U.S.C. § 1396s). Under the CDC provider rules, an enrolled provider may not charge for VFC vaccine, must screen for VFC eligibility at each visit, must waive the administration fee (capped in New York at $25.10 per dose) for families who cannot afford it, and **may not refuse to vaccinate an eligible child because of inability to pay**. If this store is a VFC-enrolled provider, the conduct described above violated those requirements outright.
- If this store is **not** VFC-enrolled, then your staff member's in-person confirmation — plus an online booking system that accepted the appointment — was a materially misleading representation to a consumer, on which I relied to my detriment. Under New York General Business Law § 349, materially misleading consumer-oriented conduct of this kind is unlawful, and willful violations carry treble damages and attorney's fees. New York's newly effective FAIR Business Practices Act additionally empowers the Attorney General to act against "unfair" and "abusive" business practices — a description the "figure it out yourself" dismissal and the public mocking of a customer fit comfortably.
- Deriding a family, out loud and in public, for seeking a federally funded childhood immunization benefit they are legally entitled to pursue is conduct I trust CVS Health does not defend. It is also the kind of verbal abuse of a patient that New York's Board of Regents rules (8 NYCRR 29.2(a)(2), applicable to pharmacy via 29.7) define as professional misconduct.

**What I am requesting:**

1. A written response and a case number, with this complaint escalated to the District Leader responsible for this store;
2. A written statement of this store's actual VFC enrollment status, and an explanation of why staff confirmed participation and why the scheduling system accepted the appointment if the store does not participate;
3. A formal apology from CVS and from the staff member involved;
4. Appropriate corrective action regarding the employees involved, and documented retraining of this store's pharmacy staff on the VFC program and on respectful treatment of patients;
5. Reimbursement of my out-of-pocket losses caused by the wasted, confirmed appointment ([transportation/lost time: $____]).

I have filed, or am filing, complaints with the NYC Department of Health and Mental Hygiene Bureau of Immunization (the VFC program authority for New York City), the New York State Education Department Office of Professional Discipline, and the New York Attorney General's Health Care Bureau. I would prefer to resolve this directly with CVS and will supplement those complaints with CVS's response — or its absence.
