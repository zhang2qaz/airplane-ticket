**Complaint narrative**

On [date], I visited the CVS Pharmacy at 2833 Broadway, New York, NY 10025 and asked whether it participates in the federal Vaccines for Children (VFC) program, which provides free vaccines to eligible children under 19. A staff member confirmed that it does. Relying on that confirmation, I booked a pediatric vaccination appointment through CVS's online system for July 30, 2026 (confirmation available).

At the appointment, the pharmacy staff member on duty denied ever having heard of VFC, demanded that I provide insurance or pay out of pocket, rudely cut off my attempt to explain the program, told me "there is no VFC here — contact VFC yourself and figure it out," and refused to vaccinate my child. No VFC eligibility screening was offered. After I stepped away, the staff member loudly mocked my family to a coworker, within earshot of other customers, as trying to get free vaccines. My child's vaccination was delayed as a result, and I incurred out-of-pocket losses ([transportation/lost time: $____]).

**Why I am bringing this to the Health Care Bureau:**

1. **Deceptive conduct (GBL § 349).** The store's in-person confirmation of VFC participation plus an online booking system that accepted the appointment was materially misleading to a reasonable consumer. Either the store participates in VFC and refused to honor the program's terms, or it does not participate and confirmed that it did. Both are misleading; the same representations face every parent who asks.
2. **Unfair and abusive treatment (FAIR Business Practices Act).** Demanding payment for a federally funded childhood immunization benefit, shifting the burden to the parent to "contact VFC and figure it out," and publicly ridiculing a family for seeking a benefit the law entitles their child to pursue is precisely the kind of unfair and abusive consumer treatment the Act now empowers your office to address.
3. **Federal program integrity.** Under CDC VFC rules, an enrolled provider may not charge for VFC vaccine, must screen eligibility at every visit, must waive the capped administration fee ($25.10 in New York) on inability to pay, and may not refuse to vaccinate an eligible child over payment. I have asked the NYC DOHMH Bureau of Immunization (the NYC VFC authority) to verify this store's enrollment status and have filed a complaint with its Provider Quality Assurance Unit, as well as with CVS corporate and the NYSED Office of Professional Discipline.

**Resolution sought:** intervention with CVS to obtain a written explanation and apology, corrective action and staff retraining, reimbursement of my out-of-pocket losses, and any broader inquiry your office considers appropriate into how CVS represents VFC participation and books pediatric vaccination appointments it does not intend to honor.
